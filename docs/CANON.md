# VEILBOUND — Canon Foundation

This file records established world and character canon. Proposed changes should be reviewed against this document before implementation.

## Core Premise

VEILBOUND is an original top-down browser action-adventure RPG set in **Eidol**, a ruined land built over an ancient underground system known as **The Vein**.

Centuries ago, The Vein powered cities, transport systems, environmental machinery, defenses, and other systems modern civilization no longer fully understands. Then a catastrophic event remembered as **The Silence** shut the network down.

Civilization survived among the ruins.

Now The Vein is waking again.

## Kael

Kael is a human masked relic hunter.

Visual anchors:
- weathered pale half-mask
- dark travel cloak
- damaged Shardblade
- ancient mechanical gauntlet fused around one forearm

Kael exists in two authored figures, and they are two speakers in the script rather than two
drafts of one:

- **Young Kael** — green hood and cloak, the Axiom set with green stones, a lighter build.
  `assets/characters/kael_portrait.png`, with `kael_keyart.jpg` showing the mask and blade.
- **Adult Kael** — dark scaled and tattered layers, a steel half-mask, the Axiom set with blue
  stones, and the Shardblade lit. `assets/characters/kael_adult_figure.png`, with
  `kael_adult_turned.png` as a second view.

The Axiom's stones read green on the younger figure and blue on the adult. Whether that is a
deliberate progression or an artefact of how the two were made is undecided; if deliberate it
is a good visible marker of the gauntlet waking, which is the kind of thing `AGENTS.md` asks
for under progression being visible on the hero.

Head-and-shoulders crops of both are the dialogue portraits. The gameplay sprite sheet is
still the placeholder prerender and matches neither.

Kael is not initially a chosen hero in his own understanding. He is a scavenger and explorer accustomed to entering dead ruins for salvage.

## Elara

Kael's mother, and the origin of the game's first scene. She is with him in a dark room as
something beats on the door; she asks him to remember her face, tells him that when the bell
rings he should follow her voice, says she is going somewhere quiet, and promises to find a
way to reach him. Then she is erased.

Her face is the thing Kael cannot recall. The opening conceals it deliberately — it is never
drawn — and Caldris names that absence as what keeps Kael from the door.

She reaches him again through the Greyhaven bell: `Kael.` and
`Do not let them make the world quiet again.`

Whether she is alive, and what "somewhere quiet" is, are open and must have a defined answer
before the reveal is authored.

## Caldris and Serac

The two voices in the void after the erasure, and the game's first antagonists. Caldris is the
decision-maker: it is Caldris who judges the removal incomplete, who is told "the child?", and
who answers `Leave him. Without her face, he will never find the door.`

Serac speaks once. Nothing else about either is established — no faction, no appearance, no
relationship to The Silence — and the temptation to make them the authors of The Silence should
be resisted until SCRIBE decides it.

"The door" is undefined and load-bearing. It is not yet known to be the Sunken Archive.

## Mira

A hunter, and the closest thing Kael has to a home. She keeps the Hunter Hall in Greyhaven,
expected him back in two weeks, and has not seen him in four months. She reads his injuries
before he admits them, and she is the one person he does not lie to well.

She is present for the first toll and for the relic breaking, which makes her the only witness
to the moment the world changes.

## Lyra — named, still undefined

Lyra stands beside Kael and Mira on the title key art and appears nowhere in the opening. She
is a woman, as Mira is and Kael is a man. Nothing else about her is canon: no role, no
relationship, no dialogue. That remains deliberate until the owner defines her.

## Kael's memory

Kael's memories of his mother were deliberately removed by Caldris and Serac when he was a
child. He does not know this. What survives is a compulsion — the bell, the vision in the mud,
`Not again.` — and the fact that something was left behind, which Caldris noticed and dismissed.

This sits underneath the existing canon rather than replacing it: Kael still understands
himself as a scavenger rather than a chosen hero, because the thing that would tell him
otherwise was taken.

## The Axiom## Orin and Tomas — named, undefined

Two further names arrived with the chibi cast in v0.4.6. Neither appears in any script, and
nothing about either is canon: no role, no age, no allegiance, no relationship to anyone
established. Their art exists; their place in Eidol does not.

The same was true of Mira for two milestones before the opening defined her. Leave them until

## The Axiom

The Axiom is the ancient mechanical gauntlet carried by Kael. It has been mostly dormant for years.

In the opening sequence, the Axiom awakens in a forgotten ruin and causes dormant systems across Eidol to begin responding.

Initial known capabilities:

### Resonance
Reveals or activates selected ancient mechanisms and hidden Vein structures. It may also expose certain enemy vulnerabilities.

### Tether
An energy tether used for traversal, object manipulation, puzzle interaction, and combat utility.

Future planned progression concepts include Impulse, Phase, Shatter, and a late-game restoration-oriented ability. These are not yet mechanically locked.

## The Shardblade

Kael's primary weapon is a damaged relic weapon called the **Shardblade**. Its conductor is fractured, leaving part of the blade incomplete and unstable.

The weapon should visibly evolve as relic components are recovered.

## Visual register

VEILBOUND is **painted**, not pixel art — high-resolution painted plates and figures, as in
the title key art, the Greyhaven plate and the Hollow March fields. Owner decision, recorded
as `ROADMAP.md` 2.14. Any earlier note or production prompt specifying pixel art is superseded.

## Eidol

Eidol is a melancholy post-collapse world where contemporary settlements coexist with remnants of infrastructure they only partially understand.

The ancient world should feel sophisticated without becoming generic science fiction. Technology is monumental, ceremonial, physical, and integrated into stone, water, metal, and landscape.

## The Vein

The Vein is an ancient underground network spanning Eidol.

Known historic functions include:
- energy distribution
- transport
- water systems
- weather/environmental regulation
- defense systems

Its deeper original purpose remains a central mystery and must be defined in the Story Bible before the corresponding reveal is authored.

## The Silence

The Silence is the name later civilizations gave the event in which Vein systems shut down simultaneously.

The public historical explanation is incomplete.

The true cause is intentionally unrevealed at this foundation stage but must eventually have a specific internal answer.

## Starting Region — Hollow March

The Hollow March is a windswept grassland built over buried ruins.

Visual/environmental anchors:
- pale tall grass
- broken stone roads
- collapsed aqueducts
- ancient machinery emerging from hillsides
- reclaimed relic components used in everyday structures
- huge sealed structures embedded in cliffs

The region begins peaceful and gradually becomes unsettling as Vein activity returns.

## Starting Settlement — Greyhaven

Greyhaven is a small settlement built around the remains of an ancient transit station.

Important locations planned:
- Wayfarer's Rest
- Relic Workshop
- Market Row
- Old Lift Station
- Archivist's House
- Bell Tower

Greyhaven should change over the course of the game: dialogue evolves, new services appear, repairs occur, and side stories progress.

## First Dungeon — The Sunken Archive

The Sunken Archive is an ancient repository beneath the Hollow March, partially flooded after The Silence.

Visual anchors:
- enormous stone halls
- turquoise water
- archive machinery
- rotating cylinders/rings
- hanging roots
- broken bridges
- luminous script beneath water
- monumental doors

Gameplay goals:
- teach basic dungeon language
- introduce room persistence and switches
- introduce water/environment interactions
- teach Resonance in authored situations
- award Tether
- prove ability reuse in traversal, puzzles, and combat

## First Boss — The Archivist

The Archivist is a massive ancient guardian machine protecting the Archive.

Current visual concept:
- stone guardian influence
- spider-like segmented locomotion
- rotating astronomical/mechanical rings
- bright central core

The fight should teach mastery of Tether and use multiple arena states.

Post-fight narrative beat currently established:

The Archivist identifies Kael as a bound user, recognizes the Axiom lineage, and finally says:

**WELCOME BACK.**

Kael has never knowingly been there before.

Why the machine recognizes him is a major story mystery and must have a defined answer before later narrative production reaches the reveal.
